# Questions — small enough to answer in a corridor

I do not study at Högskolan Väst. I am an amateur. I want the answer from someone who already knows, not a tour.

How to ask: "I have ten short questions about a sweat-ion patch versus a salt-and-sugar drink. Which three are stupid, and who on campus would actually know?"

Status key: OPEN = unanswered. Do not fill these in from a blog.

## Salt or sugar

1. OPEN — For a person who has sweated, not for cholera: when is the WHO mix (Na 75, glucose 75, K 20 mmol/L) the wrong drink, and what replaces it?
2. OPEN — Is sweat sodium a usable proxy for "how much salt to put in the bottle," or does it only say what left the skin?
3. OPEN — What single number should change the mix: sweat Na, sweat rate, body-mass loss, or none of these?

## The patch

4. OPEN — At rest, is there enough sweat to read, or does the patch only work in training unless you force sweat (iontophoresis)?
5. OPEN — For a first amateur build, is paper conductivity (total ions) enough, or is a sodium-selective electrode the minimum that means anything?
6. OPEN — What fails first on skin: reference drift, contact, or the membrane?
7. OPEN — Do I need Na and K separate, or is total ionic strength the only number a non-lab can trust?

## The loop

8. OPEN — What is the safe version: patch shows a number, human picks the drink? At what point does auto-mixing become a medical device in Sweden?
9. OPEN — Who do I ask first in Sweden — Läkemedelsverket, a sports-medicine clinic, or a sensor lab — and what one sentence should I not say?

## On campus

10. OPEN — HV is elitidrottsvänligt and has an AI and Sensor Technologies group. Who already logs something on an athlete's body (HR, lactate, sweat, force), and what should I measure with a scale and a bottle before I buy a sensor?

## Done when

Three answers written under the questions, each with a name and a date. Then stop. No station, no subsidy, no app until question 8 has a human answer.
